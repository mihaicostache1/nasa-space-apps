# Site01 terrain mask (`lunarwindow.terrain-mask.v1`)

`lola-site01-reference-mast-0m.json` is a static terrain horizon for the `lola-site01-reference` point (connecting ridge, LOLA Site01 tile centre). It is the single validated topographic demo point for LunarWindow: the other curated sites have only preliminary horizons.

## Observer

The observer is identical to the archived ephemerides in `data/reference/validation-30d/lola-site01-reference.json`.

- **Position:** lat −89.46315775697248°, east lon −137.50198490762307°.
- **Total altitude:** 1.94476708984375 km above the 1737.4 km sphere. That is the ground height of 1944.76708984375 m plus a mast of 0 m.
- **Ground height check:** the ground height is the centre pixel (row 1600, col 1600) of the original 5 m Site01 DEM. The exporter re-reads that DEM and refuses to write the mask if the ground differs from the archived altitude by more than 1 mm.
- **Frame:** the DEM frame is `MOON_ME`, DE421, using geodetic coordinates on the sphere with east-positive longitude. The archived Horizons responses report the Moon frame as `MEAN_ME (high precision)` from DE441, on the same 1737.4 km sphere. Both are Mean Earth/polar-axis frames, but they are different realizations. **No frame transformation is applied.** Whether to accept the pairing is the backend compatibility check's decision.

## How it was generated

1. **Sources.** Three NASA PGDA DEMs, each recorded with its URL, SHA-256, retrieval time, HTTP Last-Modified/ETag and CRS:
   - the Site01 5 m tile (product 78),
   - `LDEM_80S_20MPP_ADJ` at 20 m,
   - `LDEM_60S_120MPP_ADJ` at 120 m (both product 90).

   All three are south polar stereographic on the 1737.4 km sphere. The code rejects any other projection.
2. **Rays.** 3,600 great-circle rays leave the observer, 0.1° apart in azimuth, measured clockwise from local geographic north. A great circle stays in the vertical plane of its starting azimuth, so no conversion from grid north is needed.
3. **Sampling.** Samples run every 5 m (`rayStepM`) from 60 m out to 200 km (`maxDistanceM`), about 40,000 samples per ray. Heights are interpolated bilinearly from the finest DEM that covers each sample:
   - the 5 m tile, inside the square around the site that it fully covers (about 8 km),
   - then the 20 m DEM, up to 25 km,
   - then the 120 m DEM.
4. **Elevation angle.** Each sample's apparent elevation is computed exactly on the sphere (`curvatureModel: "spherical"`): `atan2((R+h)·cos δ − (R+h₀), (R+h)·sin δ)`, with `δ = d/R`. No refraction is applied.
5. **Ray horizon.** Each ray's horizon is the maximum elevation along it. Each whole-degree value is the maximum of the 10 rays within ±0.5°, which errs toward "blocked". Negative values are kept.
6. **Missing data** (`noDataPolicy: "reject"`). If any sample had no data in all three DEMs, the export would fail. None was missing: all 144 million samples have data, and every ray reaches 200 km.

## Coverage and known limitations

- **One observer only.** The mask is valid for this point, at ground level (0 m mast). Any other location or height needs a new profile.
- **A 0 m mast makes the nearest ground dominant.** The terrain that sets the horizon has a median distance of only 0.2 km, and 79% of azimuths are set within the 5 m tile. The mask is therefore sensitive to the observer setup:
  - a 2 m mast changes it by up to 1.9° (mean −0.65°),
  - including terrain from 20 m instead of 60 m changes it by up to 1.4°,
  - dropping the 5 m tier changes it by up to 0.6°.

  These sensitivities explain the lower Sun share compared with PGDA, whose 120 m maps smooth this local relief. A real lander's antenna or camera height should be agreed before drawing operational conclusions.
- **Terrain within 60 m is excluded.** This avoids treating interpolation noise at 1–10 pixels as obstruction.
- **Resolution.** 5 m pixels miss boulders and rocks smaller than a few metres. Beyond 25 km, the 120 m DEM smooths distant ridges slightly.
- **Disc model.** Whole-degree max-binning and the engine's horizontal-disc approximation are conservative simplifications. The model has no refraction (there is no atmosphere) and does not model eclipses.
- **Not yet checked against imagery.** The three QuickMap comparisons in `docs/validation/` validate Sun/Earth angles for camera observers about 100–144 km above the surface. They do **not** validate terrain shadows seen from this ground point. The PGDA comparison is a long-term plausibility check, not a check of individual sunrise or Earthset times.
